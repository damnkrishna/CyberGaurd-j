## 5. Year 1: Internship

### 5.1 Priorities (in order)
1. **Deliver at work.** Quality, on-time labs earn the PPO.
2. **Learn how INE builds labs:** environment design, vulnerable setups, reset scripts, testing, documentation.
3. **Build visible output** (labs, write-ups, detection packs). Ask INE what you may publish.
4. **Study for certs after hours.**

### 5.2 First 2 weeks
- [ ] Confirm exactly which platform and labs you can use.
- [ ] Ask HR/manager about voucher and reimbursement policy for interns.

- [ ] Learn the team's lab tooling (Docker, Ansible, Terraform/CloudFormation, AWS, VMs).
- [ ] Set up a private notes system organized by ATT&CK tactic and tool.

### 5.3 Free-time lab roadmap (about 5 hours a week)
1. **Networking and traffic (weeks 1–6):** Wireshark, TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP, reading packet captures.
2. **Linux and scripting (ongoing):** automate something small every week in Python or Bash.
3. **Web fundamentals (weeks 6–14):** PortSwigger Web Security Academy (injection, authentication, access control), then INE web labs.
4. **Exploitation (weeks 14–22):** boxes on TryHackMe or Hack The Box, privilege escalation, Metasploit.
5. **Defender view (weeks 22–30):** run an attack in your own lab with Zeek, Suricata or ELK, then find it in the data and write the Sigma rule. This is your signature skill.
6. **Keep CTFs going** at about 3–4 hours a week.

## 7. Years 3–5: Path to Security Architect

### 7.1 Skill stack
- **Offensive:** Active Directory attacks, web/API testing, post-exploitation
- **Defensive:** detection engineering, SIEM/EDR, logging architecture
- **Cloud:** identity, networking, logging, IaC security
- **Architecture:** threat modeling, zero trust, secure design reviews, risk and compliance basics
- **Engineering:** Python, PowerShell, Bash, automation, Terraform/Ansible
- **Communication:** reports, explaining risk to non-technical people

### 7.2 Moves around year 3
- **Stay at INE:** Senior Security Researcher, then technical lead (the path Ashish followed). Look for a conference Demo Lab or similar slot once you have original shipped work.
- **Switch:** bring a portfolio of CVE labs, write-ups and detection packs. Targets: detection engineering or security research at product companies, product security, AppSec, fintech/bank security, consulting.
- **Architect note:** architect roles usually want breadth in design and review experience, which often comes from security engineering roles. The research years give you depth. Plan to add design work (threat models, reviews) deliberately.
- Your goal can change. Detection engineer, researcher, and AppSec are all strong routes.

### 7.3 What makes the jump work
- Public proof: blogs, lab write-ups, detection repos, small talks
- Real labs and detections you built, not only exams passed
- Interview stories: "a problem I designed, built and defended"
- Salary: research live data about 12 months before switching
